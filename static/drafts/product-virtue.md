# Product Virtue
In my journey through computer history I continue to admire Richard Stallman's passion over the free software movement (his term for what we know as Open Source -- not actually free as in gratis). 

The "Three Cs" of Product Virtue

## Comprehension
All users should know exactly how a product functions and how it was created.

### Documentation
The purpose, shematics, production, and function of the product should be rigorously documented (or be made open source). This is to provide maximum transparency to the consumer.

### Understandability
The product and its manufacture/purpose should be possible to be understood by a single individual, if it is not, it fails to be understandable.

## Control
All users should own their inputs and outputs while using a product, and they should control the product (not the other way around).

### Effort
Users should control anything that results in their efforts using the product such as inputs, data, or labor. Example: the garden they make with a shovel, the data they input on an online forum.

### Utility
The product should serve the customer as a utility / tool, not the other way around. If a product controls the consumer without their consent or opt-out (such as alogorithms, unneccessary safeguards, etc.) the product fails to deliver itself as a tool or utility.

## Continuity
Products should maintain their original function and not be purposefully made worse.

### Support
The product should continue to be supported and run both through historical precedent and future promises.

### "Enshittification"
No product should become less useful, it should maintain its general function and purpose, if features are added, older features should not be removed or made more annoying to use.  If a product needs a sizable change, a new product should be created, rather than transforming an existing one into the new product.

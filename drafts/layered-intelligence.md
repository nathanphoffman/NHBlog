# Layered Intelligence
Something I was musing about a few days ago was the news that we had achieved "AGI" or that Swarm Agents would somehow become sentient or that these frontier models were somehow going to become the new Skynet.  Aside from the fact that modern frontier models are just doing the same thing all AI has done for years which is matrix weighing and multi-dimensional signals, I was wondering what really defines "human-like" intelligence and more so what kinds of intelligence is there?

I started to think about the problem more philosophically and came up with this as the scale:
1) **Mechanical** (Programs: Classical computation and Calculators)
2) **Pattern-Matching** (AI: Matrix/Vector weighing of known data)
3) **Intuition** (Life: Novel Real-World experience projected onto things)
4) **Abstraction** (Gods?: Relations between ideas that don't have real-world connections)
5+) **Beyond Conception** (If this tier exists, humans can't even fathom what it would be) 

This also led me to a few rules:
a) An intelligence "thinks" using the mechanics of its native layer only (so for humans that is intuition)
b) An intelligence can fully understand its own layer and those below it
c) An intelligence is supremely best at its own native layer
d) An intelligence is always exponentially slower (power for power) when operating in a non-native level
e) An intelligence can vaguely scrape the mode of intelligence one layer above it but it is unable to fully compute it
f) An intelligence can only mock the representation of the layer above through its own lower-systems (analogies in our case)
g) An intelligence can not even conceive of what would exist 2 layers above it (mechanical computers cant intuit in any sense)

## AI vs Humans
My hypothesis is that no matter how smart AI becomes it is locked inside of the "pattern matching" layer, layer #2. This means it will never fully be able to achieve true-intuition as in the ability to discover true novel information, understand "gut feeling", and "emotion," and contextualize it. This is the layer native to humans. This is why a human can see a face or a tree, or smell and instanteously *feel* an incredibly complex set of variables. We know when things feel wrong, we make judgements based on that feeling. Infact, arguably, emotion and feeling is the language of pure-intuition, something AI entirely lacks. This is why AI can say something that is absurdly wrong, when a human would immediately know the answer doesn't *feel* right. To try and emulate this feeling, AI needs to try and reach up a layer, and doing so requires near-exponential computational power, additive computation upon computation, new matrixes, etc. This is why even today AI still sometimes struggles with obvious things in AI art (five fingers, gravity, etc.).  It is also why very often humans can see AI art and somehow know it feels wrong, even if they can't explain it -- it is their intuition and judgement which comes instantly and naturally.

## A Fish vs Frontier Models
An interesting derivative that follows from this theory (especially fact C) suggests that even a fish could be as good at cutting edge frontier models (even better in someways) at intuition, as it operates natively within intuition from learned experience, at least that limited amount that falls within its domain. It may recognize something as wrong underwater, a predator appearence or something strange, that AI would struggle to identify or "feel."

## Humans vs Fish
The biggest difference between a human and a fish is that the human has enormous added processing power, leagues more, in our cerebral cortex. This allows us to build on our intuition and extend it through analogy into the abstract layer. This is most apparent in cutting edge physics such as relativity, where often to explain it you need analogies: Einstein himself used MANY analogies not just in his attempt to explain relativity, but in his attempt to understand it. What these analogies show is our human brains imperfect attempt to represent pure-abstraction (relation between unrelated things) through the things we understand (intuition related relations and reasoning). So we can't imagine time slowing down and speeding up, but we have all ridden trains, visualizing it, feeling it is instantaneous for us.  So imagining it in our head and *feeling* how a light beam would project vertically allows us to apply other experiences such as viewing trains from outside, and seeing that lightbeam move upwards.  Then when we are told the lightbeam doesn't actually slant from an apparent viewers perspective like a thrown ball might, we are blown away because that is way beyond our experience, the light to us would effectively stand still as we saw it move upwards. The fact it falls outside of any sense of learned experience or reality is what makes it difficult for us to natively see those connections, but using analogy and othe real world observation, we can slowly unravel the abstraction that underlies some of these complex ideas -- where a native abstract being (like a God) might be able to instantaneously just know relativity in the same way we "know" what the color red feels like.

It also explains something really important: when we try to explain things that don't have concrete representations within the physical world, we are guaranteed to get it at least subtly wrong (so long as my theory holds true) our brain model quite literally can't think in abstraction, so any attempt to represent it is imperfect.

## Analogy of Representation
This representation analogy extends to every layer below us as well. The mechanical layer can theoretically attempt to emulate modern AI, infact people used to do this and even call it "AI." In the 80s many companies created enormous if/else trees and branching logic manually coded to try and recognize anything a user might ask, of course doing so is highly inefficient and brute force, and frankly impossible to perfect, that it wouldn't take long for someone asking such a piece of software questions to pretty quickly figure out it isn't real AI in the pattern-matching sense.

Likewise, pattern-matching like AI of today would seriously struggle to express human intuition (as discussed). An even more interesting thought is that AI likely then can-never "feel" so long as it is stuck in the pattern-matching layer. So far all the major AI models use pattern-matching, there have been experiments to more closely resemble the human brain through like distribution of noise, etc, but they have all been extremely challenging and effective failures. 

Yet a further extension that follows based on rule G is that AI has literally no sense of what abstraction is (non-apparent connections between unrelated things based in the real world). This actually becomes pretty obvious when you realize abstraction like this needs analogy and novel experience both AI lacks, and an outside creative "gut" feeling and "dreamyness" that AI lacks to even begin to comprehend the abstract layer which is already difficult for Humans. In otherwords, it is highly unlikely that any AI could truly come up with a novel, deeply theoretical, un-real connection, or new field such as Quantum Physics or the General Theory of Relativity. Because to do so requires abstraction, and that is only even touchable by intuition, "abstraction" is not something an AI could even comprehend the concept of let alone make efforts like humans do to estimate it and imperfectly represent it.  

## A being more advanced than humanity
Yet another fascinating extension is that a being more sophisticated than a human is likely to be stuck in our own layer -- possibly a being might have brain circuitry that allows it to switch into native pattern-matching and mechanical computation modes which would give it substantial more capacity than us and they might have much larger brains allowing even faster intuition and better models of abstraction, but it is very likely their understanding of the universe would also be extremely imperfect like our own, just a bit more refined. They would undoubtedly have their own analogies, perhaps some we don't understand without understanding their society and world.

## Extending it beyond humans
There is an interesting thought that extends the theory beyond humans: what would a being be like that thought in pure abstraction? So for it the theory of relativity was as easy for it and totally graspable as the feeling of love we have when out beloved pet nuzzles us. Such a being would also, quite interestingly, struggle to intuit (in the same way we are fairly bad at pattern-matching and mechanical calculation). They would understand it, but not natively be able to feel it out like we do. The most likely analogy is that of a detached emotionless God. However, an additional problem arises, what lies beyond abstraction? A God like being like this would have to be able to comprehend what lies outside of abstraction and the way I see it, there is really only two answers: nothing lies beyond abstraction and therefore gods don't exist (as a native layer allows conception of the layer above it), gods do exist but the layer above it is literally nothing, and they know that -- thereby knowing all answers in the universe -- or gods don't exist, quite possibly because thinking in abstraction is literally not possible.
